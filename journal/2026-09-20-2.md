## What was done

The user described the first campaign in four passes, answering questions between each; every pass went into `spec.md` as a new `## First campaign — "The Governor's Term"` section between *Requirements* and *Architecture*.

- **Premise and goals.** Subsector Governor, confined to the orbital office above an edge-of-subsector capital that has its own planetary governor. Two win paths: serve out a 208-week term, or declare independence and hold the capital against the Empire's first expedition.
- **Rogue path as a switch, not a threshold.** The declaration is news that travels by dispatch or rumour; each seat reacts on receipt by dominant loyalty, shaded by temperament — player-loyal obey, Empire-loyal fight or withdraw to await the expedition, self-loyal sit it out (governors stop paying taxes; the boldest captains turn pirate) and pledge to the winner.
- **Recall is a ship, not a letter.** The successor arrives with marines and takes over the same tick. The only warning is rumour and the envoy's occasional candour. Declare while he is en route and his ship becomes a prize.
- **Five threats.** The Warlord (ex-Commodore; wants the whole subsector; recruits self-interested captains; defections surface only via rumour, resignation letters, witnesses, or the wrong side of a battle). Pirates (no faction; spawn slowly at *havens* — corrupt-governor or independent worlds with a port; each pirate carries its own haven list from spawn and is captured if it lands anywhere else; the player learns havens by rumour and by catching them). Planetary governors (any governor recalled on paper ignores it and stops paying until the term ends; replacement needs marines). Unrest (office bombed, taxes stopped, port occupied — which also cuts information). The Council (see next).
- **The Council's ears.** Standing is fed by the player's monthly report, merchant complaints, and rumour, plus tribute arriving on time. Per topic (Warlord, pirates) the Council holds a *reported truth* and a *rumoured truth*; contradiction gives a chance of the liar verdict, quoted back by the envoy. Honesty never raises standing; lying only lowers it; good months recover it.
- **Tribute and the envoy.** A monthly delegation ship with the same envoy character; the player hands over taxes and a two-section report (*under control* / *disaster, send help* per topic); aid can be requested in troops, ships, officers, or funding.
- **Five endings.** Recalled; term completed (medal at high standing); the parade (Warlord captured in his capital and standing held above a threshold); pocket kingdom; deposed. Every game ends with a ship in orbit.
- **Model consequences.** Cryofrozen troops with a standard 10% loss; appointments that travel; loyalty per seat with a neutral state; prizes (captured hulls crewed by a transported officer; destroyed hulls gone); bonus pay flips loyalty, most readily for the self-interested; no Commodore — the player appoints captains directly; one officer conversation per week at the capital yielding a written impression.
- **Elsewhere in the spec.** *Information model* gained **Rumour propagation** (merchant-borne, lane-hopping, mostly-reliable, two-way). *Characters → Learning them* gained conversation. `Report` in the data-model sketch notes rumours are reports with sub-certain fidelity. Phase 1/2 lines now name which campaign path each delivers.
- Posted the three type-shaping consequences (neutral allegiance, per-ship knowledge for pirates, `Report.fidelity`) as a comment on #11.

## Decisions (rationale in decisions.md)

- "The Governor's Term" as the first campaign, with a loyal and a rogue ending.
- Independence as a loyalty switch rather than a loyalist threshold.
- Recall as a same-tick arrival rather than a warning letter.
- Written impressions over direct trait reads for officer conversations.
