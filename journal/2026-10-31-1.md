# 2026-10-31 — Session 14: draft 2, from the vision to the letter of marque

One very long session (it ran across 2026-10-30 and 31 and through several context compactions) in which the game changed shape. The user set the scope: save the Government House game as **alpha draft 1** and start **draft 2** as a captain's career, Pirates! and Crusader Kings on a 1977-sci-fi-spaceship base, three empires sharing a borderland subsector, one life, captivity common and death rare, combat choices but two rounds at most, guild first and navy after, pirates not a separate organisation, heads giving few direct orders, the governor start gone. Four rounds of vision questions were answered and written down, and then the work went milestone by milestone with the user ruling on each question as it came up. The root was tagged `alpha-draft-1` and left alone; everything else is `draft2/`, its own package, forty-seven commits on `main`.

**The keel.** Worldgen for three empires with seats on the edge, lanes and chart, names (a Spanish pool added; seats named from their empire's core), mail with the addressee as a body and the belief fold per reader, scouts on the packets (four-week rounds so that someone can board), the stance clock, save and load, a map pane and player view that read belief and never ground truth.

**The free trader.** The economy went through three shapes: price pressure, then "milk runs pay less, gems pay more", then a physical market of stock, production and consumption with whole tons only. Duty on all trade to the governors' treasuries, smuggling with inspection, fines and arrest, docks that resell what came through them. The mortgage raised to bite (5,000 every four weeks, repossession), fuel costs plus twice-yearly maintenance in place of weekly upkeep (the user's call), ports as place lists: docks, brokers, the guild factor, the port captain, the bar. Sliders with a number box and quotes at the brokers. A dossier of prices and their age with hearsay marks. The port register, a year long, bought from the administrator, and NPCs acting on it.

**The captains.** NPC traders as agents trading on their own beliefs, then made smarter in several passes: expected prices by age and hearsay trust, mixed holds and freight, two-leg lookahead, reserves, tranche selling, crowd and rival discounts, waiting, leg memory, a two-legs-beyond circuit, home before maintenance is overdue, buying the register. No loans against cargo (the user's call). Two cliques were built, measured, and removed in favour of guild membership: who tells whom, bribes, leaks, asking, rounds offered only where they would tip a refusal, the guild's book at the seat.

**People.** Traits (greed, caution, openness, pride, loyalty), regard, trust, esteem, notes, route requests, the Book. The user asked for more ways to make friends and specified them: a good tip, standing together with anyone, route requests with tone, ransoming another, rounds, greetings weighed by significance, esteem as a second number, nine degrees of standing, no familiarity decay ("friendships and enmities persist"). Talk carries ties and, from this last commit, esteem.

**Trouble.** Robbery as a decision rather than a born class: the desperate and brave or the greedy and bold turn robber, choose a hunting ground, never rob friends, countrymen only if half-loyal, guild-fellows never. Armed traders and patrol boats as hulls, per-world danger belief, navies' patrol boats on station by danger (twelve, then nine at the user's word), outlaw raiders and havens, catching odds raised, military hulls carrying no cargo, far traders a fifth of merchants. Encounters with a round or two of choices, allies who stand with you, captivity, ransom, parole and the lender. What becomes of a hull taken: a robber robs a freighter and lets her go, takes a lesser hull for a promoted mate or shifts her flag into a better one; a navy prize sails to the seat and is condemned.

**The guild.** Bulk lines on what the book knows, haulers that saturate, lines dropped only for a better one or war, lines shown on the map. Dues of four percent set aside and paid at the seat, expulsion after a year. Fleets bought from the purse at a climbing price. Money carried, not wired: strongboxes, factors' offices, cash walking home down a tree of lines rooted at the seat, and only connected lines run (the user liked the tree and asked for it).

**The world.** Famous worlds, the imperial trade with home markets and freighters steadying each seat's prices.

**This last commit.** The yard builds hulls to order at A ports with long waits, warships only under a letter of marque (the user's addition); the court holds prizes for sale eight weeks; the marque and the guild's ticket at the player's own seat for a fee and a clean record, dues for the player with warnings; captains from the core paced by the room the seat sees; peace terms shortened so empires are at war with someone most of the time. Two bugs found by a ten-year run: ship naming looped forever on a pool's tenth reuse, and talk scanned every relation pair per conversation. A slowdown remains and was filed.
