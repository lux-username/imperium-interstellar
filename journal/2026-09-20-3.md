## What was done

**Sim.** Eight modules on top of session 2's types:

- `names.ts` — original syllable name generator for worlds and people.
- `generate.ts` — 2d6 placement at 8+ per hex (~42%), our own profile chains, capital on the edge chosen by nearby ports + population and forced to A/tech 12, a governor per populated world.
- `lanes.ts` — A/B ports joined within 2 pc, C spurs at 1 pc, clusters bridged at ≤3 pc; one packet ship per lane, some with a week's dwell (interval 2 or 4).
- `chart.ts` — routing (fewest jumps) and timetable arithmetic (`nextDeparture`, `expectedArrival`) as public knowledge, re-exported through `view.ts` so the UI can show the player the same sums.
- `mail.ts` — the whole propagation model: hand in at a port, ride the next hull along the route, transship, deliver into the reader's belief; letters to a governor's office answered by return packet.
- `game.ts` — `newGame(seed)` with a stale opening survey and week 0's sailings already made; `advanceWeek()` in a fixed order (arrivals → held mail → world drift → scheduled governor reports → sailings → the desk's direct view of the capital); `requestReport()`.
- `player.ts` — `buildPlayerView()`. `save.ts` — tagged JSON.
- Snapshots and reports now carry `governorName` / `observerName` so a governor's replacement reaches the desk only by report. `GameState` gained `nextId`.

**UI.** Three panes: inbox/outgoing tabs, SVG hex map, dossier. Advance Week, seed/new game, autosave to localStorage, save/load file. God view loaded behind `import.meta.env.DEV` via a `dev.ts` barrel; grep of `dist/` confirms none of it ships.

**Tests.** 12 → 42. Hand-built three-world line fixture pins the arithmetic (report next door lands at written + wait + 1; two lanes out transships on the timetable; a letter posted between weeks lands and is answered exactly when the envelope says). Generated-game tests: determinism over 20 weeks, save/load continues identically, every routine report in 40 weeks lands on its ETA, off-lane worlds never get mail out, the PlayerView has no truth in it.

**Playtest.** Ran it in a browser to week 15. At week 10 Coxauk's dossier said a letter would land wk 13 and the reply arrive wk 15; it did. Coxauk's routine wk-11 report took 4 weeks (missed the packet) and landed the same week as the 2-week reply — the texture the whole design is for.

## Things that went wrong and were fixed

- First generated chart had 3 A/B ports in 39 worlds and a capital with one reachable neighbour. The rules-default starport spread starves lanes; the table now leans toward B, and the capital is chosen for connectivity.
- A dispatch posted between weeks was given an ETA assuming it could catch the current week's sailings, which had already happened. ETA now starts from the following week; `newGame()` runs week 0's sailings so packets sit where the timetable says.
- Two hand-fixture tests used lane ids that didn't follow the convention; `laneBetween` now falls back to searching by ends.
- A perl substitution mangled `view.ts` (the `|` in `string | null`). Python for multi-line edits from here on.

## Decisions (rationale in decisions.md)

- Starport table weighted toward B over the rules-default spread.
- Between-week dispatches sail with the next week; week 0's sailings pre-run.
- Names travel inside snapshots; no public character roster.
- Letters are delivered to the governor's office, not the named person.
- Chart arithmetic is public knowledge, exported through `view.ts`.
