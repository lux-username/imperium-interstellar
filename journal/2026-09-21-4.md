# 2026-09-21 — Names session 3: the pools wired into worlds and people

The user asked for the names to be implemented, for planets and for officers, with the Governor and the Warlord drawing from one pool, under the 60/40 rule and the planet rule from names session 1 (`journal/2026-09-20-10.md`): every world rolls 1d3 cultures from the world table, every officer's name comes from the core table six times in ten and the world table four. That is `#49`, and STATUS said `#54` (the South Asian pools pairing Hindu given names with Muslim surnames) should land with it so the wiring draws through communities; it did.

Ran on `worktree-names-wiring` off `main` ab22f8a. `src/sim/names.ts` was rewritten: `worldCultures` (1d3, distinct), `worldName` (a toponym from one of the world's cultures, reused as "New X" or "Port X" when the bare name is on the chart), `officerCulture` (60/40), `rollSex` (even odds), and `personName`, which fills a culture's per-sex pattern with one draw per token in a fixed order — so Han, Japanese, Korean and Turkish come out family-first, Vietnamese get Văn or Thị, a Russian woman the feminine surname, the Central Asians a patronymic from a man's name, a Javanese woman a single name. `World` stores its `cultures`, `Character` its `culture` and `sex` (optional, since the player's desk and the Warlord are seats, not rolled people). `newCharacter` is the one door, so the desk's governors and officers, the Warlord's officers and pirate captains all draw from the same pool without anything changing in `warlord.ts` or `pirates.ts`. A merchant who brings a rumour to a port is named from that port's cultures rather than the officer rule — a Han world's traders are Han. The chart entry carries the cultures and the Dossier shows "Settled by". Save format 4 → 5. The syllable generator now names only ships.

For `#54` the schema grew `communities?: Community[]` (option 1 of the issue): Gangetic and Bengali split Hindu/Muslim 4:1, Deccani Marathi / Telugu–Kannada / Dakhni Muslim 2:2:1 as its notes say. The split was done by a script over the existing lists — the Muslim entries sat contiguously at the tail of every pool — with two surnames moved by hand (`Thakur` to the Bengali Hindu side, `Pillai` to the Deccani south). The flat `given`/`family` arrays are now the concatenation of the communities, so the 80/80/80 rule still applies to the file, and the test checks the partition.

The new stream broke three seed-dependent tests. None was a sim bug: seed 7's unrest event was at a world the Warlord had since taken, and the test compared the event's valence to the *current* holder; seed 12's inbox held a questioned pirate's ship-snapshot letter, legitimate since 1b, which the test predated; and the revolt-letter test counted seeds where no revolt began in three weeks as governors sitting on the news — 4 of 24 seeds, on top of ordinary 5-in-6 variance. Each was corrected to what it meant rather than re-seeded. A first probe of the names read well: Godfrey Elliott, Janet Elphinstone, Honora Higgins, Radhanath Mitra, Deng Chunlan; worlds Jingdezhen [Han], Göttingen [North German], Taiyuan [Bengali, Han, Great Russian].

Also: `.gitignore` gained a bare `node_modules` line, because a worktree that symlinks it was showing the link as untracked (`node_modules/` matches only directories). `design/culture-tables.md`'s "Use" section now describes what the code does instead of promising it.

Decisions: communities on the schema rather than new culture rows; one name pool for every side, sex at even odds, merchants named from the port (see `decisions.md`).
