# Imperium Interstellar — spec (design of record)

Changes only by deliberate decision. When a "maybe" in *Future direction* becomes real, it graduates out of this file into a Milestone with Issues.

## Goal

A solo, turn-based grand-strategy game in which the player governs a subsector on behalf of a distant Empire, where information travels only as fast as ships — so the player rules through delegation, standing orders, and a courier-borne inbox, never through direct control.

## Design pillars

These are the load-bearing ideas. A feature that undermines one of them is wrong even if it is fun.

1. **Information travels at the speed of ships.** There is no signal faster than a Jump. Everything the player sees is a *report*: an observation made somewhere, some time ago, carried here by a hull. The map is a belief state, not the world.
2. **You command through people.** Subordinates act for weeks or months on their own. Their competence, initiative, and loyalty are the gameplay. Appointments are the most consequential decisions in the game.
3. **Orders are postures, not programs.** Orders are a small set of mechanized types with a few parameters and a rendezvous — "patrol here, engage at favorable odds, then return to X" — not a scripting language.
4. **The inbox is the game.** A turn is: read what arrived, decide, write dispatches, advance a week. Packets cost money and determine how fast you can act.
5. **Isolation by mechanics.** The subsector's texture comes from 1977-era assumptions: low jump numbers, tech-level caps, charted lanes versus dark off-lane worlds, fuel risk, starport classes. Worlds are isolated from the point of view of the person in the tin can, not the god's-eye map.
6. **The player is a person with a location.** In Phase 1 that location is fixed: the desk at the subsector capital. The model treats the capital as the *delivery address*, so mobility can be added later without rewriting the information system.
7. **Original text, original setting.** Mechanics are inspired by 1977-era 2d6 science-fiction RPG design as analysed by the *Tales to Astound!* "Out of the Box" essays. No rules text is copied; no trademarks or third-party setting names appear anywhere in the game.

## Requirements

### Setting assumptions (inherited mechanics)

- **Subsector**: an 8×10 hex grid; each hex is one parsec. Roughly 30–40 worlds (about 40% hex density — deliberately sparser than the ~50% rules-default, so clusters exist).
- **Jump**: a ship with Jump-N can move up to N parsecs in one jump. Every jump takes one week regardless of distance. In-system manoeuvre is absorbed into the week.
- **Jump ratings**: J-1 and J-2 are common, J-3 uncommon, J-4 rare and expensive. The shipyard world's tech level caps what can be built.
- **Starports**: classes A–E and X. A/B: refined fuel, shipyards, regular traffic. C: unrefined fuel only. D/E: little or no fuel. X: nothing. Jumping on unrefined fuel risks drive damage and misjump.
- **Charted lanes**: regular commercial and packet traffic runs only along charted lanes between A/B (sometimes C) ports. Off-lane jumps require a capable navigator and computer, and no scheduled ship ever makes them. Off-lane worlds see a hull only when someone sends one deliberately.
- **World profile**: starport, size, atmosphere, hydrographics, population, government, law level, tech level — generated by our own 2d6 procedures producing a compact profile string.
- **Communication equals travel.** Couriers are ships. Packet ships run lanes on schedules; anything else is a ship someone chose to send.

### Core loop (one turn = one week)

1. **Inbox.** Dispatches that arrived this week are presented, each stamped with *origin date*, *origin location*, and *arrival date*.
2. **Update.** Map, roster, and ledger re-render from the player's latest reports. Every item shows its age ("as of 9 weeks ago").
3. **Act.** The player issues orders (which become outgoing dispatches on couriers), makes appointments, sets budgets, and replies to the Empire.
4. **Advance week.** The simulation resolves every entity in the subsector using *its own* knowledge and standing orders; ships move; couriers and reports advance along their routes; events fire; the player's outgoing dispatches begin travelling.

### Information model

- **Ground truth vs. belief.** The simulation holds the true state. The player's view is a set of *reports*: snapshots of an entity as observed at (location, date), delivered at (capital, later date). The map renders the newest report per entity and its age.
- **Report propagation.** An observation made at world X on week D leaves X on the next departing hull bound toward the capital (a scheduled packet on a lane, or whatever ship happens to go). Arrival = D + waiting time + transit weeks. Off-lane worlds may not report for months, or ever.
- **Dispatch propagation** (player → subordinate). Same rules in reverse. A dispatch addressed to a ship is sent to an *address* — a world or rally point. If the ship is not there when the courier arrives, the dispatch is held at the address until the ship shows up or a recall expires.
- **Subordinate knowledge.** Every acting entity (governor, ship commander, rival polity) has its own belief state and acts on it. Phase 1 may simplify NPC decision inputs, but a subordinate's *report to the player* is always what that subordinate observed, coloured by their traits.
- **Player location.** Stored as a location entity. Phase 1: always the capital. The capital's `acting_governor` slot exists from day one and holds the player.

### Orders

Few, mechanizable, with a rendezvous. Each order type is a data record, not code.

**Ship orders**
- *Move* to X by lane (or off-lane if capable), then hold / return to Y.
- *Patrol* world-or-region for N weeks with engagement posture ∈ {never, only overwhelming, favourable, even, always}; then return to rally Y.
- *Escort* a convoy or merchant route.
- *Blockade* a world.
- *Transport* troops, cargo, or personnel from A to B.
- *Courier*: carry dispatches along a route and return.
- *Survey* an off-lane world (produces a report).

**Standing orders** attached to any ship: on completion → rally point; on contact with faction F → posture; on damage beyond threshold → retreat to nearest friendly A/B port; on unexpected situation → hold and send a courier.

**Governor orders**: tax rate, garrison priority, infrastructure (starport upgrade, lane charting), unrest posture, recruitment, report frequency.

**Agent orders** (Phase 2): intelligence, subversion, protection of assets.

### Characters

Governors, ship commanders and commodores, agents, deputies, and the Empire's envoy.

- **Attributes**: competence (administrative / naval / diplomatic), initiative (cautious ↔ bold), loyalty (to the player / to the Empire / to self), ambition, and a small set of quirks.
- **Effects**: traits bias how orders are interpreted (a bold commander shifts engagement posture one step up; a cautious one retreats early), report honesty and frequency, likelihood of exceeding orders, and handling of unrest.
- **Relationships**: patronage, rivalry, faction ties. Careers: they age, die, get promoted, resign, defect.
- **Learning them**: the player only ever knows a character through their reports and outcomes. Dossiers accumulate; they are never omniscient.

### The Empire (delegation upward)

The imperial capital lies outside the subsector, roughly 12–16 weeks round trip. It sends directives, quotas (tax, ship levies), and judgement — all based on reports that were months old when read. It may reward, censure, recall, or replace the player. The player's standing with the Empire and the subsector's stability and prosperity are the game's scoring axes. Losing means recall in disgrace, or losing the subsector.

### Threats and events

Piracy on lanes; revolt on isolated worlds; a rival polity across the border; a governor's secession; misjump; plague, famine, disaster. Every event produces reports that reach the player late, and sometimes contradictory reports from different witnesses.

### Economy (kept light)

Treasury at the capital. Taxes physically travel as convoys and can be lost. Trade along lanes yields revenue at A/B ports; an interdicted lane loses it. Ships cost upkeep; couriers cost per packet. Phase 1 needs only enough economy to make packets and ships scarce.

### Combat (abstract)

Resolved by the simulation when hostile forces share a system: strength, posture, commander traits, and dice produce damage, destruction, or retreat. No tactical layer. The player learns of a battle from the commander's own after-action report, which may be self-serving.

### UI

Two primary panes: **Inbox** (dispatches with origin/arrival stamps, reply and order actions) and **Map** (SVG hex subsector with lanes, last-known markers for ships and worlds carrying age badges; click for dossier). Secondary: **Roster**, **Outgoing** (dispatches queued on couriers), **Ledger**. One **Advance Week** button. Save/load as local JSON.

## Architecture

- `src/sim/` — pure TypeScript, deterministic, no DOM. Subsector generation, entities, orders, movement, courier and report propagation, combat, events, character behaviour. Seeded PRNG; whole state serializable to JSON.
- `src/ui/` — React + SVG. Reads a `PlayerView` derived solely from delivered reports; never touches ground truth except through a dev-only "god view" toggle.
- The sim/ui boundary is strict: the UI cannot cheat, so the belief model is enforced by structure rather than discipline.
- Tests (Vitest) on the sim: determinism (same seed → same state), propagation scenarios (a report from world X arrives at week D + transit), order resolution, and character-trait effects.

## Data model / schemas

Sketch; formalized in `src/sim/types.ts` as it lands.

- `World` — hex coordinate, name, profile (starport, size, atmosphere, hydrographics, population, government, law, tech), faction, governor, unrest, garrison.
- `Lane` — two worlds, jump distance, packet schedule.
- `Ship` — class, jump rating, cargo, crew, commander, true position, current order, standing orders, own belief state.
- `Character` — attributes, traits, post, relationships, career history.
- `Order` — typed record with parameters and rendezvous.
- `Dispatch` — from, to-address, payload (order, appointment, letter), sent week, route, eta, held-at.
- `Report` — subject, observed-at location, observed week, delivered week, snapshot, observer.
- `Faction` — the Empire, the player's administration, rivals, pirates, rebels.
- `GameState` — tick, ground truth, per-agent belief states, player view, RNG state.

## Phasing

- **Phase 0 — Belief map.** Subsector generation, lanes, packet schedules, couriers, reports, hex map with age badges. No ships to command, no combat. Goal: prove that watching stale information arrive is interesting on its own.
- **Phase 1 — The desk.** Ships and orders, patrols, pirates, governors with basic traits, the inbox, Empire demands. A complete, winnable/losable loop.
- **Phase 2 — People.** Full character traits and relationships, deputies, player mobility (leaving the capital, with a deputy holding the seal), ambition and usurpation.
- **Phase 3 — Other seats.** Pocket polities and a rogue admiral as playable roles; richer economy; diplomacy.

## Out of scope

- Real-time-with-pause.
- Tactical or ship-to-ship combat.
- Any copied rules text, third-party setting names, or trademarks.
- Publishing or commercial release.

## Future direction / out of scope (for now)

Aspirational, not-yet-decided plans live here — and only here.

- Multiplayer with week-delayed diplomacy between human governors.
- Sector scale (multiple subsectors, the Empire itself on the map).
- Procedurally written narrative in reports (commanders with distinct voices).
- Mod support: alternative subsector generators and order sets.
